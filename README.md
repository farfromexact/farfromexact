I turn messy spreadsheets, market data, and quantitative rules into small products that can be inspected, challenged, and used. The common thread is simple: keep the assumptions visible and put the decision—not the dataset—at the center of the interface.

### What I build

<table>
  <tr>
    <td width="33%" valign="top">
      <strong>Market structure</strong><br><br>
      Option-chain research, regime filters, and intraday flow tools for understanding what a market is doing before deciding what to do about it.
    </td>
    <td width="33%" valign="top">
      <strong>Risk &amp; capital</strong><br><br>
      Scenario engines and portfolio-review systems that connect management questions back to auditable calculations and source data.
    </td>
    <td width="33%" valign="top">
      <strong>Learning systems</strong><br><br>
      Models and games that make difficult ideas tangible—especially when the best way to understand risk is to interact with it.
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>

### Working principles

**Make assumptions visible.** Keep sensitive data local. Treat the interface as part of the model. Prefer a small tool that can be audited to a large system nobody trusts.
